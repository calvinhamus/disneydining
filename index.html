The Dining Atlas

All dining · no dining plan required

Every restaurant, lounge, and counter worth knowing, plotted on two axes that nobody separates: food quality and immersion. At Disney these often run in opposite directions, which is how you end up paying $95 for a castle view and airline food. Four quadrants, live status warnings, and a menu panel on every entry.

Break-Even

Quick service · pure value math

Every counter scored in real dollars as entree ceiling plus drink ceiling, against the break-even your plan actually needs. Toggle the drink off and watch 50 of 88 locations drop to 2. That single gap is the entire economic case for buying the plan, and it takes one tap to see.

Quick Service

Quick service · weighted ranking

The same 88 counters, but with tunable weights for a bar on site and for a real plant-based entree. Drag the dials and the ranking recomputes live. Each bar shows where the score comes from, so you can see whether a place earns its spot on food value or on the drink list.

Snack Credits

Snacks · allocation, not ranking

One credit per night and hundreds of options, so this is an allocation problem. Set your nights, build a basket, watch the surplus. Auto-fill solves it for you. A separate view shows the traps: alcohol is never eligible, souvenir cups never count, and the turkey leg is an entree.

Drink the World

EPCOT · World Showcase · 21+

A passport for the classic lap. Pick your direction, log each pour, collect eleven stamps. It runs a standard drink count as you go, because a full lap is roughly 14 of them solo and that number belongs on screen rather than buried in a disclaimer. Splitting is the default and water stops are part of the route.

What to trust and what not to

Prices are estimates. They were built from current reporting and they move constantly. Use them to rank options against each other, not to budget to the dollar. The break-even math is only as good as the plan price you enter, so check what you actually paid.

The scores are opinions. Food and immersion ratings in the Atlas are a synthesis of critical consensus, not measured data. They encode judgments you may disagree with. California Grill sits at a 6 for food against a 9 for the room, because the room is still the best on property and the current menu has been widely criticized as coasting. Reasonable people rate that differently.

Eligibility shifts. Whether an item takes a credit can change between locations and between visits. Anything marked check the board is exactly that. Confirm in My Disney Experience before you build a day around a specific meal, and look for the purple DDP symbol on the menu before you scan.

Standard drink counts are approximations. Pour sizes at World Showcase vary widely and are not published. The counts in the passport are estimates meant to keep the total visible, not a substitute for paying attention to how you feel.

Menus are linked, not copied. Disney rotates menus constantly and publishes them per location, so every restaurant has a button that opens the current official menu rather than a snapshot that goes stale. Where specific dishes and prices were verified they are listed inline; where they were not, the panel says so instead of guessing.

Currency. Everything reflects July 2026, including closures, refurbishments, and the 2026 plan pricing. Disney's construction pace means this will drift.
